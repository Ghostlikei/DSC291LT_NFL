Setting — Batch Binary Classification

Setting

Learner. A deterministic learner maps a labeled sample to a hypothesis: $$A : (\mathcal{X} \times \{0,1\})^* \to (\mathcal{X} \to \{0,1\})$$
Population risk. $$L_D(h) \;:=\; \mathbb{P}_{(x,y)\sim D}[h(x) \neq y]$$
Realizability. $D$ is realizable if $\exists\, f$ with $L_D(f) = 0$ — $D$ is supported on the graph of $f$.
PAC learnability. $A$ PAC learns $\mathcal{H}$ if for every $\varepsilon, \delta > 0$ there exists $n$ such that for all realizable $D$: $\mathbb{P}_{S\sim D^n}[L_D(A(S)) > \varepsilon] \leq \delta$.

Main Theorem

Theorem (Batch Stochastic NFL)

Let $\mathcal{X}$ be any domain. For every deterministic learner $A$ and every $n < |\mathcal{X}|/2$, there exists a realizable distribution $D$ on $\mathcal{X} \times \{0,1\}$ such that: $$\mathbb{P}_{S \sim D^n}\!\bigl[L_D(A(S)) \geq \tfrac{1}{8}\bigr] \;\geq\; \frac{1}{7}.$$
Quantifier order. $A$ and $n$ are fixed first. Then the adversary picks a $2n$-point subset, a bad labeling, and the realizable distribution — all before the sample is drawn. This is an oblivious adversary (contrast with the adaptive adversary in the Online Adversarial setting).
Takeaway. Without structural restrictions on $\mathcal{H}$, no learner can succeed uniformly across all realizable distributions.

Proof (Four Steps)
6
Proof — Step 1: Averaging Construction

Step 1: Build a Family of $2^{2n}$ Distributions

Fix $A$ and $n$. Choose $2n$ distinct points $x_1, \ldots, x_{2n} \in \mathcal{X}$. For each labeling $b = (b_1,\ldots,b_{2n}) \in \{0,1\}^{2n}$, define:

Target. $f_b : \mathcal{X} \to \{0,1\}$ with $f_b(x_i) = b_i$ for $i \in [2n]$.
Distribution. $D_b\bigl((x_i, b_i)\bigr) = \dfrac{1}{2n}$ — uniform on the labeled graph $\{(x_i, b_i)\}_{i=1}^{2n}$.
Key observation. Every $D_b$ is realizable: $L_{D_b}(f_b) = 0$ by construction.

Now let $B = (B_1,\ldots,B_{2n})$ be a uniformly random labeling. Sampling $S \sim D_B^n$ amounts to: draw $B$, then draw $n$ indices $J_1,\ldots,J_n$ i.i.d. uniformly from $[2n]$, independently of $B$.

7
Proof — Step 2: Observed and Unseen Sets

Step 2: Lemma 1 — Unseen Points Are Unpredictable

$V(S)$ (observed). $\{i \in [2n] : x_i \text{ appears in } S\}$. Since $|S| = n$, we have $|V(S)| \leq n$.
$U(S)$ (unseen). $[2n] \setminus V(S)$. Since $|V(S)| \leq n$, we get $|U(S)| \geq n$.
Independence. Condition on $S$. The learner $A(S) = \hat{h}$ is fixed. For any $i \in U(S)$: $B_i$ was never revealed in $S$, so it is still a fair coin independent of $\hat{h}(x_i)$. $$\mathbb{E}\!\bigl[\mathbf{1}[\hat{h}(x_i) \neq B_i] \mid S\bigr] = \frac{1}{2}.$$
Lemma 1. $$\mathbb{E}[L_{D_B}(A(S)) \mid S] = \frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}[\hat{h}(x_i)\neq B_i]\mid S] \;\geq\; \frac{|U(S)|}{2n}\cdot\frac{1}{2} \;\geq\; \frac{n}{2n}\cdot\frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{4}.$$
8
Proof — Step 2 Concluded

Average Population Loss $\geq \tfrac{1}{4}$

Taking expectation over $S$ and $B$, the tower law gives:

$$\mathbb{E}_B\,\mathbb{E}_{S \sim D_B^n}\!\bigl[L_{D_B}(A(S))\bigr] \;=\; \mathbb{E}\!\bigl[\mathbb{E}[L_{D_B}(A(S))\mid S]\bigr] \;\geq\; \frac{1}{4}.$$
Core insight. A sample of size $n$ from a $2n$-point domain always misses at least $n$ points (pigeonhole). The labels at those unseen points are statistically independent of everything the learner observes — so no algorithm can predict them better than chance.
The obstacle is purely information-theoretic, not computational. No property of $A$ is used beyond the fact that it maps samples to hypotheses.
9
Proof — Step 3: Probabilistic Method

Step 3: Extract a Single Bad Labeling

We know: $$\frac{1}{2^{2n}} \sum_{b \in \{0,1\}^{2n}} \mathbb{E}_{S \sim D_b^n}\!\bigl[L_{D_b}(A(S))\bigr] \;\geq\; \frac{1}{4}.$$

Probabilistic method. If the average over all $b$ is $\geq \tfrac{1}{4}$, there must exist at least one $b^*$ such that $$\mathbb{E}_{S \sim D_{b^*}^n}\!\bigl[L_{D_{b^*}}(A(S))\bigr] \;\geq\; \frac{1}{4}.$$ Set $D = D_{b^*}$.
  • $D = D_{b^*}$ is realizable: $L_D(f_{b^*}) = 0$.
  • We have $\mathbb{E}_{S\sim D^n}[L_D(A(S))] \geq \tfrac{1}{4}$.
  • But we need a constant-probability failure event, not just a bound on the expectation.
Step 4 converts the expected-loss bound into a probability bound using a Markov-style argument.
10
Proof — Step 4: Markov Conversion

Step 4: From Expected Loss to Failure Probability

Let $Z = L_D(A(S)) \in [0,1]$ and $p = \mathbb{P}(Z \geq \tfrac{1}{8})$.

Bound $\mathbb{E}[Z]$ from above.

Decompose: on the event $Z < \tfrac{1}{8}$ use $Z \leq \tfrac{1}{8}$; on the event $Z \geq \tfrac{1}{8}$ use $Z \leq 1$:

$$\mathbb{E}[Z] \;\leq\; (1-p)\cdot\tfrac{1}{8} + p \cdot 1 = \tfrac{1}{8} + \tfrac{7p}{8}.$$
Solve for $p$.

We have $\mathbb{E}[Z] \geq \tfrac{1}{4}$, so:

$$\frac{1}{4} \;\leq\; \frac{1}{8} + \frac{7p}{8} \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; p \;\geq\; \frac{1}{7}.$$

Therefore $\;\mathbb{P}_{S\sim D^n}\!\bigl[L_D(A(S)) \geq \tfrac{1}{8}\bigr] \geq \tfrac{1}{7}.\;\square$

Why $\tfrac{1}{8}$ and $\tfrac{1}{7}$? Any threshold $\varepsilon < \tfrac{1}{4}$ works; the resulting failure bound is $\tfrac{1/4 - \varepsilon}{1 - \varepsilon}$. Setting $\varepsilon = \tfrac{1}{8}$ gives $\tfrac{1/8}{7/8} = \tfrac{1}{7}$.
11
Proof — Summary

Proof at a Glance

  • 1 Averaging construction. Fix $A$, $n$. For each $b \in \{0,1\}^{2n}$, define $D_b$ = uniform on the graph of $f_b$ over $2n$ chosen points. Every $D_b$ is realizable.
  • 2 Unseen set argument (Lemma 1). $|V(S)| \leq n$, so $|U(S)| \geq n$. For each $i \in U(S)$, $B_i$ is an independent fair coin given $S$: contribution to expected loss $= \tfrac{1}{2}$. Total: $\mathbb{E}[L_{D_B}(A(S))] \geq \tfrac{n}{2n}\cdot\tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{1}{4}$.
  • 3 Probabilistic method. Average $\geq \tfrac{1}{4}$ implies some $b^*$ has $\mathbb{E}_{S\sim D_{b^*}^n}[L_{D_{b^*}}(A(S))] \geq \tfrac{1}{4}$.
  • 4 Markov conversion. $Z \in [0,1]$, $\mathbb{E}[Z] \geq \tfrac{1}{4}$: $\;\tfrac{1}{4} \leq \tfrac{1}{8} + \tfrac{7p}{8} \implies p \geq \tfrac{1}{7}$.

Corollary — Infinite VC Dimension

Corollary: VCdim $= \infty$ $\Rightarrow$ Not PAC Learnable

Corollary 1. If $\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{H}) = \infty$, then $\mathcal{H}$ is not realizably PAC learnable (and hence not agnostically PAC learnable).

Proof sketch:

  • Fix any algorithm $A$ and sample size $n$.
  • Since $\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{H}) = \infty$, there exists a set $C = \{x_1,\ldots,x_{2n}\}$ shattered by $\mathcal{H}$.
  • Apply Theorem 1 to these $2n$ points: $\exists\, D$ with $\mathbb{P}_{S\sim D^n}[L_D(A(S)) \geq \tfrac{1}{8}] \geq \tfrac{1}{7}$.
  • Since $C$ is shattered, $\exists\, h^* \in \mathcal{H}$ matching the bad labeling: $L_D(h^*) = 0$, so $D$ is realizable by $\mathcal{H}$.
  • With $\varepsilon = \tfrac{1}{8}$, $\delta = \tfrac{1}{7}$: PAC condition fails for every $n$. $\square$
Fundamental Theorem of PAC Learning: $\mathcal{H}$ is PAC learnable $\iff$ $\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{H}) < \infty$. NFL provides the hard direction.

Lean 4 — Core Statements

Key Lean 4 Definitions and Theorem

-- Population risk  L_D(h) = ∫ 1[h(x) ≠ y] dD
noncomputable def risk (D : Measure (X × Bool)) (h : X → Bool) : ℝ :=
  ∫ xy, (if h xy.1 = xy.2 then 0 else 1) ∂D

-- D_b = uniform on graph of f_b (Step 1)
noncomputable def graphPMF (C : Finset X) (f : X → Bool) (hC : C.Nonempty)
    : PMF (X × Bool) :=
  PMF.uniformOfFinset (graphFinset C f) (graphFinset_nonempty f hC)

-- Step 2: average over labelings gives risk ≥ 1/4 (Lemma 1)
theorem sum_abstractRisk_ge (S : Fin m → Z) ... :
    (Fintype.card (Fin m → Z) : ℝ) / 4 ≤
      ∑ f : Z → Bool, abstractRisk e f (A (abstractSampleList e f S))

-- Step 3+4: some labeling has failure probability ≥ 1/7
theorem exists_abstractFailureRate_ge (C : Finset X) ... :
    ∃ fZ : Z → Bool, (1 : ℝ) / 7 ≤ abstractFailureRate e A fZ m

-- Main theorem
theorem batch_stochastic_NFL
    [Fintype X] [MeasurableSingletonClass X] [Nonempty X]
    (A : Learner X) (m : Nat) (hm : 2 * m ≤ Fintype.card X) :
    ∃ D : Measure (X × Bool), IsProbabilityMeasure D ∧
      (∃ f : X → Bool, risk D f = 0) ∧
      (1 : ℝ) / 7 ≤ failureProb D A m (1 / 8)

Batch Stochastic No-Free-Lunch — Summary

What We Showed

  • For any learner $A$ on a domain with $|\mathcal{X}| > 2n$, there exists a realizable $D$ with $\mathbb{P}_{S\sim D^n}[L_D(A(S))\geq\tfrac{1}{8}]\geq\tfrac{1}{7}$.
  • The hard distribution is uniform on $2n$ points — the unseen half (at least $n$ points) is the source of failure.
  • Proof: averaging construction → $V(S)/U(S)$ independence argument → probabilistic method → Markov conversion.
  • Corollary: $\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{H}) = \infty \Rightarrow$ not PAC learnable (hard direction of the Fundamental Theorem).
  • Machine-verified in Lean 4 / Mathlib.
Contrast with Online Adversarial NFL. Online: adaptive adversary flips label after seeing each prediction. Batch: oblivious adversary fixes $D$ before any sample. Both conclude the same thing, via different mechanisms: adaptive fooling vs. information-theoretic blindspot.
Three NFL theorems, one message. Without inductive bias — online, search, or batch PAC — learning is impossible in the worst case.
