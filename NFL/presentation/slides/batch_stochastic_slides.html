Setting — Batch Binary Classification

Setting

Learner. A deterministic learner maps training data to a hypothesis: $$A : (\mathcal{X} \times \{0,1\})^* \to (\mathcal{X} \to \{0,1\})$$
Risk. 0-1 loss under distribution $D$: $$R(D, h) = \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim D}[\mathbf{1}[h(x)\neq y]]$$
Failure probability. $$\delta(D, A, m) = \Pr_{S \sim D^m}\!\bigl[R(D, A(S)) \geq \tfrac{1}{8}\bigr]$$
Realizability. $D$ is realizable if there exists $f$ with $R(D, f) = 0$, i.e., $D$ is supported on the graph of $f$.

Main Theorem

Theorem (Batch Stochastic NFL)

Let $\mathcal{X}$ be a finite domain with $|\mathcal{X}| \geq 2m$. For any deterministic learner $A$, there exists a realizable distribution $D$ on $\mathcal{X} \times \{0,1\}$ such that: $$\delta(D,\, A,\, m) \;=\; \Pr_{S \sim D^m}\!\bigl[R(D,\, A(S)) \geq \tfrac{1}{8}\bigr] \;\geq\; \frac{1}{7}.$$
Takeaway. No matter how clever the learner, there is always a realizable distribution for which it fails with probability $\geq 1/7$.

Contrast with Wolpert–Macready: here the target is fixed before sampling (not adversarially adaptive). The difficulty comes from the information-theoretic blindspot of seeing only $m$ of $2m$ points.

Proof

Proof — Zero-Sample Case

Case 1: $m = 0$

Strategy. With no training data, $A([])$ is a fixed hypothesis — fool it at a single point.
  • Pick any $x_0 \in \mathcal{X}$; set $b := \lnot A([])(x_0)$.
  • Define $D := \mathrm{Dirac}(x_0, b)$ — a point mass at the adversarial pair.
  • $D$ is realizable: the constant $b$ has risk 0 under $D$.
  • The unique 0-sample training set is $[]$; $A([])$ predicts $\lnot b$ at $x_0$, so $R(D, A([])) = 1 \geq 1/8$.
  • The $D^0$-measure of the failure event equals 1, so $\delta(D, A, 0) = 1 \geq 1/7$.
Lean 4: batch_stochastic_NFL_zero — uses Measure.dirac, integral_dirac, Measure.pi of the empty product = 1.

Proof — Setup for $m \geq 1$

Case 2: $m \geq 1$ — Choosing the Support

Since $2m \leq |\mathcal{X}|$, pick a finset $C \subseteq \mathcal{X}$ with $|C| = 2m$.

Graph distribution. For any labelling $f : C \to \{0,1\}$, define $$D_f \;:=\; \mathrm{Uniform}\!\bigl(\{(x, f(x)) : x \in C\}\bigr)$$ — the uniform distribution on the graph of $f$ over $C$. This is realizable (risk of $f$ under $D_f$ is 0).

Let $\mathcal{F} = \{0,1\}^C$ (all $2^{2m}$ labellings) and $\Omega = C^m$ (all training samples of size $m$ from $C$).

Goal. Show: some $f \in \mathcal{F}$ has combinatorial failure rate $\dfrac{|\{S \in \Omega : R(D_f, A(S)) \geq 1/8\}|}{|\Omega|} \;\geq\; \dfrac{1}{7}$.
8
Proof — Fixed-Sample Lower Bound

Lemma: Average Risk $\geq \tfrac{1}{4}$ per Sample

Lemma. For any fixed sample $S = (z_1,\ldots,z_m) \in \Omega$: $$\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}|}\sum_{f \in \mathcal{F}} R(D_f,\, A(\mathrm{sample}(S,f))) \;\geq\; \frac{1}{4}.$$

Proof sketch:

  • $S$ sees at most $m$ distinct points from $C$ (with $|C| = 2m$).
  • Let $U$ = unseen points: $|U| \geq m$.
  • For each unseen $z \in U$: pairing $f$ with its label-flip $f^z$ at $z$ shows loss$(f) +$ loss$(f^z) = 1$ at point $z$. Total contribution to average risk $\geq 1/(2m)$ per unseen point.
  • With $|U| \geq m$ unseen points: average risk $\geq m \cdot \frac{1}{2m} \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{4}$.
9
Proof — Double-Counting Contradiction

Contradiction via Double-Counting (Fubini)

Suppose for contradiction that every $f \in \mathcal{F}$ has failure rate $< 1/7$. Consider the double average: $$\Sigma := \frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}||\Omega|} \sum_{f \in \mathcal{F}} \sum_{S \in \Omega} R(D_f, A(S))$$

Upper bound. If failure rate $< 1/7$: on bad samples use bound $\leq 1$, on good samples use bound $< 1/8$: $$\Sigma < \frac{1}{7} + \frac{6}{7}\cdot\frac{1}{8} = \frac{8+6}{56} = \frac{14}{56} = \frac{1}{4}$$
Lower bound. Swap $f$ and $S$ (Fubini), then apply the fixed-sample lemma: $$\Sigma \;=\; \frac{1}{|\Omega|}\sum_S \underbrace{\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}|}\sum_f R_f(S)}_{\geq\,1/4} \;\geq\; \frac{1}{4}$$
Contradiction: $\Sigma < 1/4$ and $\Sigma \geq 1/4$ cannot both hold. So some $f^* \in \mathcal{F}$ must have failure rate $\geq 1/7$.
10
Proof — Measure-Theoretic Bridge

Bridging to the Continuous Setting

We have a combinatorial failure rate over $\Omega = C^m$; we need a measure-theoretic statement about $D_{f^*}^m$.

Measure Bridge Lemma. The combinatorial failure rate $\leq$ the measure-theoretic failure probability: $$\frac{|\{S \in \Omega : R(D_f, A(S)) \geq 1/8\}|}{|\Omega|} \;\leq\; \Pr_{S \sim D_f^m}\!\bigl[R(D_f, A(S)) \geq \tfrac{1}{8}\bigr]$$
  • Bad combinatorial samples embed injectively into the $D_f^m$-failure event via the support-trace embedding.
  • Each embedded sample gets mass $= 1/|\Omega|$ under $D_f^m$ (by uniformity of $D_f$ on the $|C| = 2m$ graph points).
  • Measure is preserved: $\Pr_{D_f^m}[\text{failure event}] \geq$ failure rate.
Pick $f^*$ from Step 3. Then $D_{f^*}$ is realizable and $\delta(D_{f^*}, A, m) \geq 1/7$.
11
Proof — Summary

Proof at a Glance

  • 1 $m = 0$: Dirac at a single point labelled adversarially.
  • 2 Pick support $C \subseteq \mathcal{X}$ with $|C| = 2m$; define $D_f$ = uniform on graph of $f$ over $C$.
  • 3 Fixed-sample lower bound: for any $S$, average risk over all $f \geq 1/4$ (unseen points contribute independent label pairs).
  • 4 Contradiction: if all failure rates $< 1/7$, double-counting gives $\Sigma < 1/4$ and $\Sigma \geq 1/4$.
  • 5 Bridge: combinatorial failure rate $\leq$ measure-theoretic failure probability via support-trace embedding.

-- Deterministic learning algorithm
abbrev Learner (X : Type*) := List (X × Bool) → (X → Bool)

-- Measure-theoretic risk
noncomputable def risk (D : Measure (X × Bool)) (h : X → Bool) : ℝ :=
  ∫ xy, (if h xy.1 = xy.2 then 0 else 1) ∂D

-- Failure probability
noncomputable def failureProb
    (D : Measure (X × Bool)) (A : Learner X) (m : Nat) (ε : ℝ) : ℝ :=
  (Measure.pi (fun _ : Fin m => D))
    {S | ε ≤ risk D (A (List.ofFn S))}.toReal

Batch Stochastic No-Free-Lunch — Summary

What We Showed

  • For any learner $A$ on a domain with $|\mathcal{X}| \geq 2m$, there exists a realizable $D$ with $\delta(D, A, m) \geq 1/7$.
  • The hard distribution is uniform on a graph of size $2m$ — the unseen half is the source of failure.
  • The proof uses a clean double-counting (Fubini) argument: average-risk-per-sample $\geq 1/4$ contradicts failure-rate-$<1/7$.
  • Machine-verified in Lean 4 / Mathlib.
Why $|\mathcal{X}| \geq 2m$? We need a support of size $2m$ so that any training sample of size $m$ leaves at least $m$ unseen points. This is the minimal condition for the fixed-sample lower bound.
Three NFL theorems, one message. Without inductive bias — online adversarial, stochastic search, or batch PAC — learning is impossible in the worst case.
